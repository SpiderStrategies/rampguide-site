01Why you will need onethe program's own words, in plain English

The package used to be a file. Now it is a place.

FedRAMP's 2026 rules describe how a cloud provider must share its certification package with the agencies that rely on it. Four things are required, and none of them is optional on the 20x track.

  • Access on requestAn agency asks, you decide. Government customers and prospects request access. Access is granted, not open: agency staff are provisioned on arrival from a known government domain or wait for your approval, and every viewer is a person your compliance owner can see, revoke, and report on.
  • The current packageWhat they retrieve is the real thing. The machine-readable package and the readable documents, at the version you have published, with an inventory of what is there: name, summary, size, version, last updated.
  • A log of everythingEvery access is recorded and reportable. Who requested, who approved, who viewed what, when. The grants list doubles as your access inventory.
  • Denials reportedA refusal is an event, not silence. Denied requests are logged with a reason. When the request came from a federal agency, FedRAMP must hear within five business days: the Access page gives you the report's text and the deadline, and keeps both in view until you record FedRAMP's ticket number.

The dates, rule set by rule set.

FedRAMP's 2026 rules take effect one rule set at a time. A new certification must follow a rule set once its date has passed; an existing one has a date to adopt it by and, after that, a grace period under a corrective action plan. The ones that matter most here:

  • VDR · VERVulnerability Detection and Response, and Vulnerability Evaluation and Reporting: required 2026-12-07, for new and existing certifications alike. An existing certification that misses it keeps going only under a corrective action plan, and only until 2027-03-07. FedRAMP's notice on the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency's Binding Operational Directive 26-04 (BOD 26-04) says a provider that follows these two rule sets meets the directive's timelines.
  • CDSCertification Data Sharing, the trust center rules: optional since 2026-07-04. Required for a new Rev5 certification from 2027-01-01. An existing certification should adopt them by 2027-08-01 and must by 2028-02-01.
  • MASMinimum Assessment Scope: required for a new Rev5 certification from 2027-01-01. An existing certification adopts it at its first independent assessment started after 2027-01-01.
  • SCN · CPO · SDRThe machine-readable package: required for a new Rev5 certification from 2027-01-01. An existing certification should adopt the Significant Change Notification rules by 2027-01-01 (must by 2027-06-01), the Certification Package Overview by 2027-07-01 and the Security Decision Record by 2027-08-01 (each must by its first independent assessment started after that date).
  • 20xOn the 20x track, a new certification needs all of these now, except the vulnerability rules, which are required from 2026-12-07 there too.

From fedramp.gov, read 2026-09-28: the Rev5 deadlines, the 20x deadlines, and FedRAMP's response to BOD 26-04. FedRAMP changes these pages; check them before you plan around a date.

02How it worksfrom the workbench to every read

Press Publish. Your readers have the new package.

There is nothing to upload and nothing to keep in sync. The package on your trust center is exactly what the last build made from the answers your people committed, and each document's date comes from its own change history.

01

Build

Your people answer and commit in the workbench; a build turns the committed answers into the package. No AI writes any of it.

02

Publish

An owner publishes from the Publish page. Each file carries a tier, so a reader sees only what theirs allows: the machine-readable package, the readable documents, the document inventory.

03

Decide who reads

Requests land on the Access page. An owner lets each one in at a tier or turns it down; readers sign in with a passkey. Federal agency staff can be let in automatically. Remove anyone the same way.

04

Every event logged

Views, downloads, approvals, denials, with the package version each viewer saw. The same change history becomes your quarterly Ongoing Certification Report.

Two things come with building it this way. The document inventory FedRAMP asks for comes out of every build, with each document's version and last-updated date. And the readable documents and the machine-readable package can never disagree on the trust center, because both came out of the same build. Programs read the same package through a documented API.
