RampGuide's trust center is that window, live at trust.rampguide.com and fed directly by your builds. It sits on the same host as the app your people build the package in, so publishing is one step, not an integration.

01Why you will need onethe program's own words, in plain English

The package used to be a file. Now it is a place.

FedRAMP's 2026 rules describe how a cloud provider must share its certification package with the agencies that rely on it. Four things are required, and none of them is optional on the 20x track.

  • Access on requestAn agency asks, you decide. Government customers and prospects request access. Access is granted, not open: agency staff are provisioned on arrival from a known government domain or wait for your approval, and every viewer is a person your compliance owner can see, revoke, and report on.
  • The current packageWhat they retrieve is the real thing. The machine-readable package and the readable documents, at the version you have published, with an inventory of what is there: name, summary, size, version, last updated.
  • A log of everythingEvery access is recorded and reportable. Who requested, who approved, who viewed what, when. The grants list doubles as your access inventory.
  • Denials reportedA refusal is an event, not silence. Denied requests are logged with a reason. When the request came from a federal agency, FedRAMP must hear within five business days: the Access page gives you the report's text and the deadline, and keeps both in view until you record FedRAMP's ticket number.
02How it worksa rendering of your repo

Publish a build. That is the whole integration.

Other platforms' trust centers render their database. RampGuide's renders your git repo: the package on the trust center is the same bytes your last build produced, with dates and word counts computed from your history.

01

Build, as always

Your people answer and commit in the workbench; a build turns the committed files into the package. No AI and no network while it runs.

02

Publish

An owner publishes from the Publish page. Each file carries a tier, so a reader sees only what theirs allows: the machine-readable package, the readable documents, the document inventory.

03

Decide who reads

Requests land on the Access page. An owner lets each one in at a tier or turns it down; readers sign in with a passkey. Federal agency staff can be let in automatically. Remove anyone the same way.

04

Every event logged

Views, downloads, approvals, denials, with the package version each viewer saw. The change history is the quarterly report you already generate.

Two things fall out of building it this way. The inventory FedRAMP asks for already comes out of every build, word counts and last-updated dates included. And the readable documents and the machine-readable package can never disagree on the trust center, because both came out of the same build. Programs read the same package through a documented API.

Run by us, one trust center per offering.

Each offering gets its own trust center on trust.rampguide.com, beside the app its package is built in. You decide who reads it; RampGuide runs the service and never decides who gets in. The package itself is plain files in a git repository we store for you, and a full copy is yours whenever you ask.

Inside your boundary
The command line · your environment

The build can stay inside your boundary.

Teams whose authorization boundary requires it run the same compiler as a command line in their own environment and publish the result to their trust center with a publish key. What agencies read is still exactly the bytes that build wrote.

04Honest statuswhat exists, what does not

Live. Not finished.

Live at trust.rampguide.com since 2026-09-23: passkey sign-in, requests and approvals, automatic entry for federal agency staff, tiers, the logged package, the access inventory and daily summaries, the denial report to FedRAMP, a snapshot per Ongoing Certification Report, and an API for programs.

Not built yet: the status page FedRAMP asks for at Class B and above (its rule allows it to be separate), an upload slot for FedRAMP's own certification reports, and sign-in with a government smart card (a Personal Identity Verification card or a Common Access Card). RampGuide itself is not FedRAMP authorized.
