RampGuide is a hosted app. Your people give it the facts about your system in the workbench: which tools you use, how they are configured, who is responsible for what. Its compiler writes the whole FedRAMP package from those facts, and rewrites it whenever a fact changes.

Cloud and SaaS companies pursuing a FedRAMP authorization, or keeping one, on either of FedRAMP's tracks: the classic Rev5 baseline or the new, faster 20x track. And the consultants and assessors who help them.

04The paperwork problemand why it is getting worse

Selling to the government starts with a mountain of paperwork.

When one fact changes, someone hunts it down in dozens of places. They always miss some.

Before an agency can use your cloud product, FedRAMP, the government's security approval program, makes you prove you keep their data safe. The proof runs hundreds of pages in a format that is picky on purpose, and it must stay current forever. Most companies still write it by hand, in Word.

The target keeps moving. The program now wants the package machine-readable, not just printable. Rules get revised. Baselines transition. Cryptographic-module certificates lapse on a schedule nobody chose. Content that was right last quarter is quietly wrong this one.

RampGuide's answer: keep each fact in one place and generate everything else from it. The same facts always produce the same package, so nothing in it changes unless someone changed a fact, and every change carries a name and a date. No AI writes your documents.

The words on this page, in plain English
  • FedRAMPThe U.S. government's security approval program for cloud products. No approval, no federal customers.
  • ATOAuthority to Operate: the approval itself, granted after your package is assessed.
  • SSPSystem Security Plan: the big document describing how your system meets every security requirement. Usually hundreds of pages.
  • Rev5 / 20xFedRAMP's two tracks. Rev5 is the classic one, built on NIST's 800-53 controls at three levels: Low (156), Moderate (323) and High (410); RampGuide builds Moderate today. 20x is the new, faster track built on 46 Key Security Indicators.
  • Machine-readable packageThe data files (JSON) FedRAMP now requires alongside the readable documents, so the government can process submissions automatically.
  • ConMonContinuous monitoring: the quarterly reports, change notifications, and vulnerability reports you owe the program after you are authorized.
  • Assessor (3PAO)The independent third party that checks your package and your system before authorization, and every year after.
  • Trust centerThe secure website where government customers request and review your package. Required on 20x now; on the classic track, for new certifications from 2027-01-01 and existing ones by 2027-08-01.

09The trust centerlive at trust.rampguide.com

Where your package lives once it exists.

FedRAMP no longer wants your package emailed around. Agencies request access, someone approves, they retrieve the current package, and every access and every denial is logged and reportable. On the 20x track this window is required from day one; on the classic Rev5 track, new certifications need it from 2027-01-01 and existing ones by 2027-08-01.

RampGuide's trust center is that window, live at trust.rampguide.com: an owner presses Publish, and the package, the readable policies, and the document inventory land there, every file hashed. You decide who reads. Federal agency staff with a .gov or .mil address on the federal list kept by CISA, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, can be let in automatically; everyone else waits for your approval. Every read is logged, and programs read the same package through the API.
